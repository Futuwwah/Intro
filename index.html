WHAT IS FUTUWWA?

The Path of the Noble Man

What is Futuwwa?

Futuwwa started around a thousand years ago as practical Islamic ethical guidelines for Muslim youth.

It is drawn from the lives of the Prophets — from Adam to Muḥammad (pbuh) — and their followers in all ages.

The great scholar of hadith, who authored the first known Kitāb al-Futuwwah, Abd al-Raḥmān al-Sulamī of Nīshāpūr (325–412 AH / 936–1021 CE), defines Futuwwah as:

Futuwwah means following the ordinances of perfect devotion, leaving all evil, and attaining in action and in thought the best of visible and hidden good conduct. Every condition and every moment demand from you one aspect of Futuwwah. There is no state or time without that demand. There is futuwwah fit for your behavior toward God, another toward the Prophet, and others toward his companions; yet others toward the pure ones of the past, your sheikh, your brotherhood, and the two angels on your shoulders who keep the accounting of your deeds.

Who is it for?

The futuwwah ethics is not only for the youth but for all ages and walks of life, because it is used synonymously with murūwwah — the wholesome personality.

It is also not only for men but for women as well, because good morality cannot be thought exclusive to young men. In sum, it is a universal morality for all, regardless of age and gender.

The core values of futuwwah are universal even beyond religion and civilization.

Example: Bushido in Japanese culture — a parallel code of honor, courage, and self-mastery.

Central Aspects of Futuwwa

Fata (Young Man) came to mean the ideal, noble, and perfect man whose hospitality and generosity would extend until he had nothing left for himself — a man who would give all, including his life, for the sake of his friends.

According to the Sufis, Futuwwah is a code of honorable conduct that follows the example of the prophets, saints, sages, and the intimate friends and lovers of Allah.

Futuwwah is a state of mind. It means placing other people above oneself. It is being generous and altruistic. It is self-denial, immunity to disappointment, indulgence toward other people’s shortcomings. It is a fearless struggle against tyranny — and above all, it is love.

Why Futuwwa?

The purpose of Futuwwa is to put the principles of ethics in the Qur’an and the Sunnah into practice.

The Qur’an and the Sunnah may seem vast and inaccessible to many. Futuwwah acts as a digest of these diverse principles.

Its goal is to develop the most wholesome personality (murūwwah) and the highest level of morality (makārim al-akhlāq), originating from the teaching and practice of Prophet Muḥammad (pbuh).

How to Put Principles into Practice

Futuwwah ethics utilizes the stories of great figures and saintly persons — similar to the Qur’anic and hadith narratives that convey moral messages through stories of the past.

Futuwwah chooses its role models from the Prophets, the Companions of Prophet Muhammad (a.s.m.), and saintly men and women throughout Islamic history. We follow their footsteps.
